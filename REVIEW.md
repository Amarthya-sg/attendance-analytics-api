# REVIEW.md


| # | Where (function / line) | What is wrong | How you'd notice it (test, input, or symptom) | How you fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `compute_late_minutes`, L36-37 | It floors to whole minutes and then checks `> 10`. R2 says late means strictly more than 10:00 after shift start, so the check has to be on seconds. | A punch-in at 09:40:01 returns 0 but should return 10. 09:40:00 and 10:15:59 come out right, which is why the happy path hides it. | Compare total seconds against 600. If late, return `seconds // 60`. |
| 2 | `compute_late_minutes` L35, `compute_overtime` L46 | Neither helper handles overnight shifts (R1). Late measures from a shift start on the punch's own day, and overtime measures from a shift end on the record's date instead of the next day. | Shift 22:00-06:00: a punch-in at 01:00 IST gives late 0 (should be 180), and a punch-out at 06:40 the next day gives overtime 1480 (should be 40). | When `shift_end <= shift_start`, the shift start goes on the previous day if the IST punch-in is before `shift_end`, and the shift end goes on the next day. |
| 3 | `MongoClient` L22, `compute_late_minutes` L35, `compute_overtime` L47, `create_employee` L82 | The client isn't `tz_aware`, so every date read from Mongo is naive. After that `replace(hour=...)` works in the wrong zone (UTC or server-local, not IST), subtracting an IST-aware `end` from a naive `punch_out` raises `TypeError`, and `datetime.now()` is saved as if it were UTC. | A UTC-aware instant of 09:41 IST gives late 0 instead of 11. `compute_overtime` on a value read back from Mongo raises `can't subtract offset-naive and offset-aware datetimes` (a 500). On a non-UTC server, `created_at` is off by the UTC offset. | `MongoClient(..., tz_aware=True)`, every instant converted to IST before building shift times, and `datetime.now(timezone.utc)` for `created_at`. |
| 4 | `compute_late_minutes`, `compute_work_hours`, `punch_in` L102 | Instants are never cut to whole seconds (R1). Microseconds feed the calculations and get stored. | 09:40:00.900 should count as 09:40:00 (late 0) and be stored and returned truncated. | `replace(microsecond=0)` on every incoming instant before calculating or storing. |
| 5 | `compute_work_hours`, L41 | `round(float, 2)` rounds the binary float, not half-up (R4). | 54 seconds of work (0.015 h) gives 0.01 instead of 0.02. 162 seconds (0.045 h) gives 0.04 instead of 0.05. | Work from whole seconds and round with `Decimal(...).quantize(Decimal("0.01"), ROUND_HALF_UP)`. |
| 6 | `compute_overtime`, L47 | The 30-minute minimum from R3 is missing, so any positive difference comes back. | A punch-out at 18:45 (15 minutes past shift end) returns 15 instead of 0. | Return the floored minutes only when they are at least 30, otherwise 0. |
| 7 | `health`, L72-74 | It always returns 200 and never talks to MongoDB. The contract wants 200 only when Mongo answers a ping, and 503 otherwise. | Stop mongod and call `/health`: it still says 200. | Ping inside try/except and raise `HTTPException(503)` on failure. Also set a short `serverSelectionTimeoutMS`. |
| 8 | every return in `create_employee`, `list_employees`, `punch_in`, `list_attendance` | The response shape doesn't match the contract in three ways. Datetimes come out as ISO strings instead of integer epoch milliseconds (also inside `history`). `punch_in` and `list_attendance` leak `_id` as `id`. Legacy documents without `history`, `half_day`, `late_minutes` or `overtime_minutes` come back without those required fields. | `created_at` returns `"2026-10-09T10:12:03.123456"`. The response has an `"id"` key. Sample #9 (the legacy record) has no `history` or `half_day`. | One serializer used by every endpoint. It converts instants to epoch ms (nested history too), drops `_id` and fills defaults (`history=[]`, `half_day=False`, counters `0`). |
| 9 | `create_employee` L79-83, `punch_in` L104-118 | Both do `find_one` and then `insert_one`, which is a race, and neither collection has a unique index. The contract wants the database to enforce uniqueness and exactly one 201 under concurrency. | Send 10 parallel POSTs with the same `emp_code`, or 10 identical punch-ins: several get 201 and duplicates land in the collection. | Unique index on `employees.emp_code` and on `attendance_logs (emp_code, date)`, created at startup. Insert directly and turn `DuplicateKeyError` into 409. |
| 10 | `EmployeeIn`, L53-60 | Every field is a plain `str`. The contract has an `emp_code` pattern (`EMP` plus 4-6 digits), an email pattern, length limits, `HH:MM` shifts, `shift_start != shift_end` and a real date for `joined_on`. | POST `{"emp_code":"x","shift_start":"25:99","joined_on":"nope",...}` returns 201. | Pydantic `Field` patterns and lengths, a validator that checks `joined_on` is a real date, and a model validator for `shift_start != shift_end`. All of them give 422. |
| 11 | `list_employees`, L93 | `skip = page * page_size` should be `(page - 1) * page_size`. | `page=1` returns the second page, and the first page can't be reached. | `skip = (page - 1) * page_size`. |
| 12 | `list_employees`, L94 | `count_documents({})` ignores the department filter, but R10 says `total` reflects the filters. | `?department=Engineering` returns 3 items while `total` is the size of the whole collection. | `count_documents(q)`. |
| 13 | `list_employees`, L95 | There is no sort. The contract wants `emp_code` ascending, and skip/limit without a sort isn't deterministic anyway. | Employees inserted out of order come back in insertion order, and pages can overlap. | `.sort("emp_code", 1)`, which the unique index backs. |
| 14 | `list_employees` L89, `list_attendance` L130-131 | `page` and `page_size` have no limits. R10 wants `page >= 1` and `1 <= page_size <= 100`. | `page=0` gives a negative skip (a 500 in employees) or the slice `[-20:0]` (attendance). `page_size=100000` is accepted. | `Query(1, ge=1)` and `Query(20, ge=1, le=100)`. |
| 15 | `punch_in`, L101, L113 | `emp` is never checked for `None`, so `emp["shift_start"]` raises `TypeError` and returns a 500. The contract says 404. | POST a punch-in for an unknown `emp_code`. | Return 404 first, before any date or duplicate logic. |
| 16 | `PunchInIn` L65-66, `punch_in` L102 | The body isn't validated. `punched_at: Optional[int]` accepts strings (`"1783312500000"`), whole floats, `true` and seconds-sized values, and `if body.punched_at` treats `0` as "not given". `status: str` accepts `ABSENT`, `LEAVE` and garbage. The contract says 422 for all of these. | `punched_at: "1783312500000"` or `1783312500` returns 201. `punched_at: 0` quietly uses "now". `status: "LEAVE"` creates a record with punch times. | A strict int limited to `100000000000..4102444800000`, an `is not None` test, and `Literal["PRESENT","WFH","ON_DUTY"]` for `status`. |
| 17 | `punch_in`, L102-103 | The attendance date comes from `ts.date()` on a naive server-local time, not the IST calendar date (R1), and the overnight rule (a punch-in before `shift_end` belongs to the previous day) is missing. | A punch-in at 23:50 IST on a UTC server gets the wrong date. An overnight punch-in at 01:00 IST gets today's date instead of yesterday's. | Convert to IST, then derive `date` from the employee's shift and the overnight rule. |
| 18 | `list_attendance`, L144-147 | It loads every matching document into memory, then sorts and slices in Python. It can't use an index and gets slow around 100k records. | `explain` shows a full collection scan, and latency and memory grow with the collection. | `find(q).sort(...).skip(...).limit(...)` plus `count_documents(q)`, with compound indexes that match the filters and the sort. |
| 19 | `list_attendance`, L145 | It sorts by `date` descending only. The contract wants `date` descending, then `emp_code` ascending, and Python's stable sort leaves ties in insertion order. | Two employees on the same date come back in arbitrary order. | `.sort([("date", -1), ("emp_code", 1)])`. |
| 20 | `list_attendance`, L125-143 | `date_from`, `date_to` and `status` aren't validated. Bad date strings pass, `date_from > date_to` isn't rejected, and `status` can be any string. The contract says 422. | `?date_from=2026-07-10&date_to=2026-07-01` returns 200 with empty items. `?status=foo` returns 200. | Check the `YYYY-MM-DD` format, require `date_from <= date_to`, and limit `status` to the five allowed values. |
